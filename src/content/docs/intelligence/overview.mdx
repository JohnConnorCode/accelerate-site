A generic assistant can draft a plausible-sounding follow-up email. An assistant that can actually read the opportunity, the conversation history, and what your team already promised can draft the follow-up that is actually correct, and explain which records it used to write it. That difference is the whole reason AI in Command Center is built to read through your registered workspace tools rather than working from a blank context window.

Open **AI Workspace** to start a conversation. A connected workspace needs its AI provider configured first; the public demo simulates the experience without making a real model request, so you can see the shape of it before connecting anything.

## Start with a question that has a useful next step

Try "What needs my attention today?" first, then narrow it: "Show me this opportunity and explain why its next action is overdue." A genuinely useful answer names the records it actually read and is honest about the limits of that evidence, so you can open the source yourself and decide what to do next rather than taking the summary on faith.

For a request that changes something, name the customer or record and the exact change you want: "Prepare a follow-up for this conversation" or "Propose moving this opportunity to the next stage." What the assistant can actually do depends on the tools and permissions your workspace has enabled; the [tool catalog](/docs/intelligence/tools) is generated straight from the registered tools, not written as a marketing list of aspirations.

## From a request to a recorded result

<DocsApprovalLoop />

A read can answer you immediately, since nothing about it changes a record. A supported write instead prepares a proposal that waits for your review: inspect the affected record and the exact change in [Approvals](/docs/command-center/approvals), approve it once it's right, then check the result afterward. That loop is what lets AI help with real operational work while the decision, and its outcome, both stay visible to you.

If a source is missing or a connection is down, a well-behaved answer says so rather than guessing around the gap. [Action results](/docs/start/receipts) explains how to tell a prepared draft, an approved request, and a completed effect apart, which matters whenever you're deciding whether to retry something that might have already happened.

## Adding the capability your business actually needs

A custom AI tool can expose one narrow business operation: read a service history, prepare a renewal plan, propose a project update. Each one still needs real implementation and verification behind it, and once it exists, both your in-app assistant and any connected external assistant can use it through the same registered operation.

[Plugin examples](/docs/plugins) show the range of what that looks like in practice. Business Pulse computes its findings without a model call at all. Client onboarding prepares tasks for your approval. [Opportunity Radar](/docs/plugins/opportunity-radar) goes further, adding a full review workspace with its own evidence and model-budget controls.

## What to check

- The [MCP connection guide](/docs/extend/mcp-clients) explains how to bring your preferred external assistant into the same workspace. Connecting a client grants exactly the capabilities available to that workspace, never unrestricted access to every service or database behind it.
- For pipeline and revenue analysis, pair AI with the [analytics guides](/docs/pipeline/revenue) rather than relying on either alone: a recorded amount, a forecast, and a genuine gap in the data mean three different things, and AI should help explain that evidence, not quietly turn an estimate into a confirmed payment.
