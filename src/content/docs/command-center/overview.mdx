A customer's message, the deal it belongs to, what your team promised them, and the next step: those four things usually live in four different places, which is exactly why the person who picks up the phone is always starting from scratch. Command Center keeps them together, so your day starts with the work that actually needs a decision instead of a search across your inbox, your CRM, and your memory.

[Try your first workflow](/docs/start/daily-path) in the fictional demo to see this before you set anything up. You will review a proposed action and check its result without connecting a single account.

## Where the work actually happens

**[Today](/docs/command-center/today)** is where your day starts: replies waiting on you, tasks due, and proposals ready for a decision, ranked with the reason each one is there. **[Inbox](/docs/command-center/inbox)** is what to triage before it becomes urgent, new inquiries and messages that still need an owner. When you are ready to answer someone, **[Conversations](/docs/conversations/reply)** has the thread and a composer that already knows who you are replying to and what deal they are attached to.

Moving a deal forward happens on the **[Pipeline](/docs/pipeline/board)** board, where every card carries its stage, its value, and the people connected to it. **[Contacts](/docs/contacts)** is where you find a person or company and see everything linked to them in one place. When your assistant has prepared something, **[Approvals](/docs/command-center/approvals)** is where you decide whether it runs, and **[Activity](/docs/command-center/activity)** is where you go afterward to confirm it actually happened. **[Ask AI](/docs/command-center/ask)** answers a specific question using whatever the workspace can see, and shows you the records it used to answer it. **[Work](/docs/command-center/work)** is the filtered task-and-approval list underneath all of it, the same records as Today, organized for editing rather than triage.

## Approval today, less approval tomorrow

The assistant reads whatever context your workspace has connected and can prepare a proposed change: a drafted reply, a suggested stage move, a task. You review that proposal through the same approval screen every time it happens, at first. What makes this worth building into your day is that it does not stay "every time": once you have approved the same kind of action three times over at least a week, the system proposes letting that specific action run on its own from then on, and you decide whether to grant it. [Approvals](/docs/command-center/approvals) explains exactly how that graduation works and which actions never graduate.

A manual action you take yourself, like writing and sending a reply from the conversation screen, follows that screen's own confirmation step and never touches this queue at all. After anything runs, check its result: prepared, approved, sent, and delivered are four different states, and a draft sitting in the queue is not the same thing as a message a customer has actually received.

## What your workspace can actually do

What you see depends on which modules are turned on and which providers you have connected and verified. Seeing a capability listed in the [reference](/docs/command-center/capabilities) tells you the product supports it; it does not tell you your workspace has connected that provider yet. [Setup Center](/docs/workspace/setup) is where to check before you promise a live workflow to your team.
