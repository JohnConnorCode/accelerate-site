Command Center is useful when customer context and follow-up are scattered across people and tools. Start by proving that one recurring piece of work becomes easier to manage.

You need a person who owns the business process and a person who can operate the installation. They can be different people. Exploring the demo needs neither provider accounts nor technical setup.

## Decide whether it fits

A good first use is answering enquiries, keeping proposals moving, or tracking commitments after a meeting. Choose work that has a clear owner, a visible next action, and a result you can check.

If your problem is already solved by one small integration, keep that option on the table. A shared workspace adds value when the same customer context needs to support several decisions or actions.

[Try the fictional workflow](/docs/start/daily-path) before committing to an installation. Ask whether the queue and approval steps match how your team actually works.

## Run a small pilot

For example, a fictional studio wants every new enquiry to have an owner and a next step.

1. **Write the starting point.** Count enquiries waiting for a reply and note how the team currently follows up. Record how you measured it so you can compare later.
2. **Choose one owner.** Name the person who reviews Today and the person who handles connection failures. Agree when each checks the workspace.
3. **Prepare the workspace.** Follow [Setup Center](/docs/workspace/setup). Verify the selected business context and the connections needed for this workflow.
4. **Rehearse with controlled data.** Open a conversation, prepare a reply, review its recipient and content, and check the resulting activity. Use a mailbox your team controls for a real sending test.
5. **Start with a small, agreed set of real work.** Import only the records needed for the pilot. Review uncertain matches before relying on customer context.
6. **Review after a week.** Compare outstanding replies, missed commitments, time spent reviewing, and failed or uncertain actions. Expand only if the team can explain the results.

An AI draft is work to review. An approved action still needs a result. A provider accepting a message does not prove the customer received it. [Check an action result](/docs/start/receipts) explains those differences.

## Agree who pays and who maintains it

Self-hosting means someone operates the application, database, backups, updates, and provider accounts. Budget for hosting, database usage, email delivery, AI usage if enabled, and maintenance time. This guide does not quote a bundled subscription or promise a support response time.

Before adoption, write down who owns each account, who approves spend, where failures are reported, and who restores service. Ask your implementer for the installed version and a demonstrated recovery process.

## Know what is ready

The demo uses fictional data. Connected features depend on enabled modules, workspace permissions, and verified providers. Consult the [capability reference](/docs/command-center/capabilities) and your own Setup Center before promising a workflow to your team.

The platform vision includes broader agent execution. Treat a proposed future capability as a planning item until it exists in your installation and has been tested for your workflow.
