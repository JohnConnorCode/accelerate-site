Your team has customers waiting, work in progress and money to follow up. The owner’s job is to understand which situation needs a decision and who should act next. Command Center connects that review to the actual conversation, opportunity, client or invoice, so a useful finding can become owned work while its context is still in view.

## Start with the business review

Open **Business overview → Today**. **Business review** organizes inspected evidence into Sales, Customer follow-up, Delivery and Money. Each finding explains the situation and opens the source where you can act. Read the timestamp and any source warning alongside it, then choose the item whose next action matters most to your business.

An opportunity’s estimated value tells you about potential sales. For money already owed or collected, open the invoice and its recorded payment status through **Billing & payments**. Collections contributes bounded payment follow-up when enabled, and a missing connection or failed read remains visible instead of becoming a reassuring total.

## Give the next step an owner

Use **Customers & sales** to understand the relationship, **Client work** to inspect delivery, and **Messages & marketing** to prepare a response. Open **Tasks & approvals → Work** when a decision needs an assigned task or a proposed action needs review. The source link stays with the work, so the person receiving it can understand why it exists.

Ask AI beside a finding prepares a question with that source identified. The assistant can inspect the current context and propose supported next steps for review. Read the saved result in the affected record, then refresh Today to see the available evidence again.

## Practice a complete job

Choose [answer an inquiry, start client work or prepare an invoice](/docs/start/daily-path). Each walkthrough begins with a business situation and ends with a result you can reopen, plus a recovery path when something fails. **How this works** on a page explains its sequence, while the visible **Start here** instruction helps you make the first useful move.

If one focused workflow already solves your team’s problem, use that as the starting point. A connected workspace becomes valuable when the same customer history supports several decisions across the week. The [builder reading path](/docs/extend) explains how a technical owner can adapt it around your operation.

## Run a small pilot

Northline Roofing & Exteriors, one of the fictional demo businesses, wants every new inquiry to have an owner and a visible next step within a day. Here is the shape of a real pilot built around that goal.

<StepByStep>
  <Step number={1} title="Write down the starting point">
    Count inquiries currently waiting for a reply and note how your team follows up today. Write
    down how you measured it, so you have something real to compare against in a week.
  </Step>
  <Step number={2} title="Choose one owner">
    Name the person who reviews Today each day and the person who handles a connection failure when
    one happens. Agree on when each of them checks the workspace.
  </Step>
  <Step number={3} title="Prepare the workspace">
    Follow [Setup](/docs/workspace/setup) and verify the business context and connections this
    specific pilot needs, not every connection the product supports.
  </Step>
  <Step number={4} title="Rehearse with controlled data">
    Open a conversation, prepare a reply, review the recipient and the content, and check the
    resulting activity. Use a mailbox your team controls for a real sending test before you touch a
    live customer.
  </Step>
  <Step number={5} title="Start with a small, agreed set of real work">
    Import only the records the pilot needs. Review every uncertain match the system proposes before
    you rely on that customer's context for a real decision.
  </Step>
  <Step number={6} title="Review after a week">
    Compare outstanding replies, missed commitments, time spent reviewing, and any failed or
    uncertain actions against your starting point. Expand only if your team can explain the results,
    not just point at a number that moved.
  </Step>
</StepByStep>

## Who pays and who maintains it

Self-hosting means someone on your side operates the application, the database, backups, updates, and every provider account you connect. Budget for hosting, database usage, email delivery, AI usage if you enable it, and the maintenance time itself; this guide does not quote a bundled subscription because there is not one, and it does not promise a support response time.

Before you adopt it for real, write down who owns each account, who approves new spend, where a failure gets reported, and who is responsible for restoring service. Ask whoever implements it for you which version is installed and ask them to demonstrate an actual recovery, not just describe one.

## What to check

- The demo uses fictional data throughout. What is connected in your workspace depends on your enabled modules, your permissions, and your verified providers, so consult the [capability reference](/docs/command-center/capabilities) and your own Setup before you promise a workflow to your team.
- Broader autonomous agent execution is part of the platform's direction, not something every installation has today. Treat a capability you have read about but not tested in your own workspace as a planning item, not a commitment you can make on its behalf.
