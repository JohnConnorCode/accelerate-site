If a new inquiry can sit for two days because the person who should answer it does not know it arrived, or if the same customer question gets a different answer depending on who takes the call, Command Center is solving a real problem for you. If your team already has one clear system of record and one clear owner for follow-up, it may not be.

You need two people, though they can be the same person: someone who owns the business process being improved, and someone who can operate the installation, meaning connect accounts, watch for failures, and fix them. Exploring the demo needs neither a provider account nor any technical setup at all.

## Decide whether it actually fits

A good first use has a clear owner, a next action you can point to, and a result you can check afterward: answering inquiries, keeping proposals moving, or tracking commitments after a meeting all qualify. If your problem is already solved by one small, focused integration, that is a legitimate answer and you should keep it on the table. A shared workspace earns its keep when the same customer context needs to support several different decisions across the week, not just one.

[Try the fictional workflow](/docs/start/daily-path) before committing to a real installation, and while you are in it, ask whether the queue and the approval step actually match how your team works today, not how you wish it worked.

## Run a small pilot

Northline Roofing & Exteriors, one of the fictional demo businesses, wants every new inquiry to have an owner and a visible next step within a day. Here is the shape of a real pilot built around that goal.

<StepByStep>
  <Step number={1} title="Write down the starting point">
    Count inquiries currently waiting for a reply and note how your team follows up today. Write
    down how you measured it, so you have something real to compare against in a week.
  </Step>
  <Step number={2} title="Choose one owner">
    Name the person who reviews Today each day and the person who handles a connection failure when
    one happens. Agree on when each of them actually checks the workspace.
  </Step>
  <Step number={3} title="Prepare the workspace">
    Follow [Setup Center](/docs/workspace/setup) and verify the business context and connections
    this specific pilot needs, not every connection the product supports.
  </Step>
  <Step number={4} title="Rehearse with controlled data">
    Open a conversation, prepare a reply, review the recipient and the content, and check the
    resulting activity. Use a mailbox your team actually controls for a real sending test before you
    touch a live customer.
  </Step>
  <Step number={5} title="Start with a small, agreed set of real work">
    Import only the records the pilot needs. Review every uncertain match the system proposes before
    you rely on that customer's context for a real decision.
  </Step>
  <Step number={6} title="Review after a week">
    Compare outstanding replies, missed commitments, time spent reviewing, and any failed or
    uncertain actions against your starting point. Expand only if your team can actually explain the
    results, not just point at a number that moved.
  </Step>
</StepByStep>

## Who pays and who maintains it

Self-hosting means someone on your side operates the application, the database, backups, updates, and every provider account you connect. Budget for hosting, database usage, email delivery, AI usage if you enable it, and the maintenance time itself; this guide does not quote a bundled subscription because there is not one, and it does not promise a support response time.

Before you adopt it for real, write down who owns each account, who approves new spend, where a failure gets reported, and who is responsible for restoring service. Ask whoever implements it for you which version is installed and ask them to demonstrate an actual recovery, not just describe one.

## What to check

- The demo uses fictional data throughout. What is actually connected in your workspace depends on your enabled modules, your permissions, and your verified providers, so consult the [capability reference](/docs/command-center/capabilities) and your own Setup Center before you promise a workflow to your team.
- Broader autonomous agent execution is part of the platform's direction, not something every installation has today. Treat a capability you have read about but not tested in your own workspace as a planning item, not a commitment you can make on its behalf.
