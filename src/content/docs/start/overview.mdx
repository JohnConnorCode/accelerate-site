Your business already has a way of working: the people you serve, the promises you make, the tools you rely on, and the decisions that need your attention. Command Center brings that context into a workspace where your team and AI can work with the same records.

You can review a deal, understand its conversation history, ask AI what needs attention, and prepare the next action without rebuilding the context each time. As your needs grow, you can add your own screens, integrations and plugins. The source is available under the MIT license; you can host the application with your own database and provider accounts.

## What changes when your tools share context?

Imagine a customer asks when their project can start. The useful answer depends on more than the message: who they are, which proposal they accepted, and what your team has promised. Linked contacts, conversations, opportunities and tasks give you a place to find that context and keep the next step attached to the right customer.

AI uses the workspace's available tools to read that information and prepare supported changes. You can inspect the sources, review a proposed action, and check its recorded result. The [AI guide](/docs/intelligence) shows how to move from a question to useful work.

## Start with a working foundation, then shape it

The application includes customer records, conversations, pipeline, campaigns, proposals, tasks and analytics. Optional modules let you choose the areas you need. Connections bring external information into the workspace; extensions add business-specific behavior.

The [plugin examples](/docs/plugins) make this tangible. Business Pulse combines several sources into a report. Client onboarding turns an approved plan into assigned tasks. Collections connects invoice evidence to a reviewed reminder. Each example explains what runs today and what you could build on top of it.

A coding assistant can help implement those extensions using the same records, permissions and action services as the existing application. You still review and test the result. An extensible foundation gives you room to build; it does not make an unimplemented integration appear automatically.

## Explore it before connecting your business

Open the [fictional demo](/demo/command-center), then follow [your first workflow](/docs/start/daily-path). It needs no account or provider keys, and its actions stay inside the demo. Look at Today, open a customer conversation, and inspect an approval to see how the pieces connect.

When you are ready to use your own data, follow [installation](/docs/self-hosting/installation) and [workspace setup](/docs/workspace/setup). You control the deployment and database. Connected providers still process the information you send them under their own terms, and hosting, email and model usage can incur costs.
