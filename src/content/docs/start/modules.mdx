A business should be able to enable the capabilities its team needs and keep optional screens out of the way. Modules control those areas of the workspace, while shared core capabilities remain available to support the connected records and workflows.

A small set of core modules, command, pipeline, conversations, contacts, intelligence, and system, stay on for every workspace, because the rest of the product assumes they exist. Everything else is optional, and what you enable is what your team sees.

## What happens when you turn one off

Turning off a module is not cosmetic. Its navigation entries disappear from the sidebar, so nobody stumbles into a screen that will not work. Its admin routes show a disabled notice instead of the real page if someone bookmarks a direct link. Its API routes refuse the request outright rather than silently doing nothing. And its AI tools are marked unavailable to both the assistant and any external MCP client, so the assistant will not attempt an action your workspace has not turned on.

The Integrations screen is the one deliberate exception: it can always re-enable a module, because gating Integrations itself would strand an operator with no way back once something got turned off by mistake.

## Where to change it

Open **Integrations**, then choose **Pluggable Modules**. Each card shows the module's status, owner and autonomy policy. Enable an optional module to make its supported screens available. Expand **Settings** to change its configuration, or open **Documentation** for its setup and operating instructions.

The same cards appear on **Plugins**, where you can open a business workflow or run an enabled report. A completed report adds a timestamped system entry to its card. If an owner or execution receipt has not been supplied, the card says so. An empty activity line does not establish that a module has never run.

The autonomy label describes the module's declared policy. It does not grant permission or change an action's approval requirements. See [Workspace](/docs/workspace) for the rest of the configuration screen.

## Adding one that does not exist yet

A third party registers a new module as a manifest under `extensions/` in the source, rather than editing the core application directly. That keeps your changes separate from the parts of the product everyone shares. The full contract for what a manifest needs is in the repository's extending guide, and its identity card appears through the generated registry. The extension check requires a documentation URL and a bundled operator guide. Review the guide and follow its workflow before release; that structural check does not test a live page.
