The fastest way to burn a relationship is to keep emailing someone after they have already replied, unsubscribed, or told you to stop, and the fastest way for that to happen by accident is automation that does not actually check before it sends. Outreach is campaigns, email templates, and reactivation of past demand, built around that one non-negotiable rule: contacts who unsubscribed or asked to be left alone are refused before a send is even attempted, every time, and a sequence stops the moment someone replies.

## Where each screen fits

[Campaigns](/docs/outreach/campaigns) is where you approve a version once, and from there automation runs entirely inside the sender, audience, cadence, limit, and stop rules you set. [Email Studio](/docs/outreach/email-studio) is where you edit live copy, inspect exactly what already went out, and compose a direct one-off follow-up. [Revenue Recovery](/docs/outreach/recovery) turns a reviewed past relationship, a no-show, a stale quote, into a governed opportunity worth another conversation instead of a name you've forgotten about.

## Collections, if invoicing is enabled

[Collections](/docs/outreach/collections) brings verified overdue invoices, payment promises, disputes, and reviewed reminders into one workspace. Enable it in Plugins and connect invoicing and email before relying on it for real accounts.

## What each piece actually guarantees

A campaign is versioned copy plus a membership list plus a send policy, and activation is itself an approved action, not something that happens by editing a draft. Email Studio keeps drafts protected and publishing explicit, and its preview and test sends use the exact same rendering path as production, so a test that looks right is genuinely the template that will send. Recovery keeps stale opportunities, no-shows, and past quotes in a working set, and its playbooks propose work into the same [approval](/docs/command-center/approvals) queue as everything else in the product, never a separate, less-reviewed path.
