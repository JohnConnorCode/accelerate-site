The gap between "the product could theoretically support this" and "here is a working example you can actually open" is usually where extending software gets hard, because you're guessing at patterns instead of copying one that already runs. These eleven bundled examples close that gap: they ship in the source, disabled by default, and each one's own guide explains exactly what it does today, how to try it, and which parts you'd actually adapt for your business.

## Reports that turn records into a useful review

Four examples compute a factual finding without ever calling a model, which is worth noticing: [Business Pulse](/docs/plugins/business-pulse) combines pipeline risks, overdue commitments, and upcoming meetings into one report. [Pipeline follow-up](/docs/plugins/pipeline-watch) surfaces open deals with a stale update or an overdue next action. [Overdue commitments](/docs/plugins/commitment-watch) lists overdue tasks linked back to the work behind them. [Meeting preparation](/docs/plugins/meeting-prep) flags upcoming meetings that actually need prep time. Together they're a useful pattern for keeping a factual business rule in plain code while still letting AI help interpret what the report found.

## Workflows that create something you can use

Three examples connect a preview to an approval and an execution result, the same loop the rest of the product runs on. [Client onboarding](/docs/plugins/client-onboarding) turns a won opportunity into a reviewed checklist. [Meeting commitments](/docs/plugins/meeting-commitments) turns a meeting into assigned, dated tasks. [Stripe invoicing](/docs/plugins/stripe-invoicing) covers reviewed invoice creation, sending, and customer presentation. Each shows how your own workflow can reuse the platform's task or provider services instead of building them again from nothing.

## Workspaces built around a business process

Two examples go further and become a small business process on their own. [Receivables Collections](/docs/plugins/receivables-collections) brings invoice evidence, payment promises, disputes, and reviewed reminders into one case workspace. [Opportunity Radar](/docs/plugins/opportunity-radar) combines a business profile with source-backed growth and relationship reviews. Both guides are explicit about what's finished today and what the larger extension pattern looks like.

## Learning the smallest building block

The [Inventory registration example](/docs/plugins/example-inventory) demonstrates a page, navigation entry, and settings, and nothing more; it's a scaffold for learning module registration, not an inventory product you'd actually run.

## Building your own

Choose an example close to what you want, read its guide, and follow the linked source. Describe your desired business outcome to a coding assistant along with the records it should use and the approvals it has to preserve, then implement and test a focused change using [the extension guide](/docs/extend). A good extension feels like part of the same workspace, the same customers, the same permissions, a result your team can actually inspect, and that shared foundation is what frees you to spend your effort on the part that's genuinely specific to your business.

## Going beyond the examples

These eleven are a starting library, not a ceiling. You can build an App with its own records, a custom lifecycle, its own queue, and a bespoke interface. Follow [the customization path](/docs/customize) to choose between a settings change and a source change, or use [the AI development brief](/docs/extend/ai-authoring) to describe a new App to a coding assistant. General-purpose App creation inside Command Center itself is planned but not shipped; today's path runs through the source repository. [Site Studio](/docs/plugins/site-studio) prepares private, template- or AI-assisted page drafts with versioned database storage; it starts disabled and does not publish pages on its own.
