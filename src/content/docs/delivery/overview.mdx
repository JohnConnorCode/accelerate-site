Winning a deal is the easy part to track; delivering on it well, over months, with nothing dropped, is the part that actually determines whether that client renews. Delivery is what happens after a deal closes: client records, bookings, content operations, and the resource library, all in one place instead of scattered across whatever tool each function happened to be using.

[Clients](/docs/delivery/clients) holds active accounts, their retainer scope, and delivery status. [Bookings](/docs/delivery/bookings) tracks qualification, scheduled calls, attendance, and attributed revenue. [Content](/docs/delivery/content) keeps editorial briefs and publishing status on a shared calendar. [Resources](/docs/delivery/resources) is your gated downloads, attached to the contacts who requested them.

## What each screen actually shows you

Clients, at `/admin/clients`, is the delivery view of people and companies you already have in the system, showing active accounts, retainer scope, and delivery status together. Bookings covers meeting records, including calendar sync when that integration is turned on; Calendly stays optional and off until your workspace enables it, so nothing about delivery depends on it by default. Content and Resources both attach to contacts and campaigns when those records exist, which means a resource download is not just a file served, it creates real intake you can follow up on.
