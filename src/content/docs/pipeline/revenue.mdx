An opportunity's value, a signed proposal's total, and money that has actually landed in your bank account are three different numbers that happen to share a currency symbol, and confusing them is how a business ends up planning payroll around revenue it hasn't collected yet. Open `/admin/revenue` to review recorded client value and accepted proposals; this screen calculates every figure from your client and proposal records specifically, not from cash a payment provider has confirmed collecting.

## What the figures mean

| Figure                    | Source in the current application                              |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Monthly recurring revenue | Monthly values on active client records                        |
| One-time value            | One-time values recorded on client records                     |
| Average client value      | Active-client monthly total divided by the active-client count |
| Industry breakdown        | Active-client monthly values grouped by industry               |
| Accepted proposal value   | Monthly totals on accepted proposals                           |

The MRR timeline is derived from client status and contract-start dates, falling back to record creation dates when a start date is missing. Treat it as a view of your recorded client data, useful for spotting a trend, rather than an immutable ledger of every invoice, payment, and contract change that ever happened.

## Investigate a difference

1. Identify the figure and the records included in it. Compare monthly value with monthly value; keep one-time amounts separate.
2. Open the relevant [client](/docs/delivery/clients) or [proposal](/docs/proposals) and check status, amount and dates against the agreement.
3. Compare with [Pipeline](/docs/pipeline/board) only after checking what that view counts. An opportunity amount, a client agreement and an accepted proposal can represent different stages of the same business.
4. Correct a demonstrated source-record error through its supported edit flow, then reload Revenue.

Resist the urge to edit a record just to make two reports agree with each other; if the underlying inputs are actually correct and the calculation still comes out different, that is worth reporting, with the expected formula and a couple of example record IDs, to your technical owner rather than papering over.
