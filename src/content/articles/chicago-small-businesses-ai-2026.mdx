A useful AI project starts with a recurring business problem: an inquiry waiting for a response, a quote missing important details, or an agreement that never becomes assigned work. Chicago small businesses can start with one of these workflows and judge the result against their own baseline.

## Choose one process worth improving

Look at the last few times the process ran. Identify who received the request, where the information went, which decision required a person and how the team knew the work was complete. An example with missing details is often more useful than an ideal process diagram.

Good first candidates have a clear owner, a repeated sequence and an observable result. For example, a catering request can become a reviewed opportunity with a menu decision pending. A property management agreement can become a checklist with named owners. These examples describe possible configurations, not customer results.

## Practical projects for different businesses

### Restaurants and catering

Capture event date, guest count, budget range and delivery needs together. Have the catering lead confirm menu feasibility and dietary requirements before quoting. Once the event is agreed, assign access and preparation checks. See the [catering inquiry recipe](/docs/recipes/catering-inquiry) and [event handoff recipe](/docs/recipes/catering-event-handoff).

Keep POS orders and kitchen production in the systems that own them. Connecting those systems is a separate integration project with its own permissions and completion checks.

### Property managers and cleaning companies

An owner inquiry needs property scope and a desired start date. A cleaning quote needs site context, service frequency and access arrangements. In both cases, staff should confirm scope before promising capacity or price.

The [property management guide](/industries/property-management) and [cleaning company guide](/industries/cleaning-companies) show how to prepare an agreed engagement for delivery. Tenant records, rent collection and crew scheduling require appropriate specialist systems or separately built integrations.

### Salons, studios and pet-service businesses

A consultation or trial request should give staff enough context to make the next decision. It should not promise availability before the team checks the service calendar. Explore the [salon consultation](/docs/recipes/salon-consultation), [fitness trial](/docs/recipes/fitness-trial) and [pet-service inquiry](/docs/recipes/pet-service-inquiry) recipes.

Collect only what the review needs. Keep health, treatment and animal records in the authorized system that manages them.

### Retailers, repair shops and staffing firms

Wholesale requests need product quantities and delivery requirements. Repair inquiries need vehicle context and professional review. Staffing inquiries need role scope and an employer decision-maker. The [industry directory](/industries) connects each business type to a worked inquiry recipe and a delivery handoff.

## Choose the right implementation

A focused improvement might use your existing tools, a custom integration, an internal screen or team training. [Command Center](/command-center) is useful when contacts, opportunities, commitments and reviewed actions need shared context. It is optional when working with Accelerate.

The platform recipes combine existing features and plugins. Plugins need to be enabled and configured in a workspace you control. Industry-specific booking, inventory, payroll and professional record systems are separate implementation work unless a tested connection already exists in your installation.

Before approving a custom connection, identify the source of truth, the permitted data, the person responsible for approval and the receipt that proves completion. Specify what happens when the provider is unavailable or a request is retried.

## Set a baseline before the pilot

Choose one measure that represents the problem. Examples include the time from inquiry to an assigned next action, opportunities without a next step, or handoffs missing an owner. Record how the team measures it so the comparison stays consistent.

Run the workflow with fictional records first. Then use an agreed, limited pilot with authorized business data. Compare the actual result with the baseline and ask the people doing the work whether the workflow reduced effort or simply moved it elsewhere. Avoid translating estimated time savings into revenue claims without evidence.

## Agree on cost and ownership

Ask what the project includes: workflow design, integration development, data preparation, training, hosting and ongoing support. Provider usage and AI model costs depend on the configuration. Agree on who reviews failures, handles changes and maintains the connection after launch.

A documented handoff should name the owner, explain how to verify a result and show how to recover safely. This matters as much as the first successful demonstration.
