You do not need a polished project or a list of technical questions to attend an AI workshop. A small task from your working day gives you something concrete to practice and makes it easier to recognize a useful result.

For our November 11 workshop in Chicago, bring your laptop and one task you would like to make easier. Here is a simple way to choose it.

## Pick a task you understand

Think about something you do often enough to explain in a few sentences. Good starting points include:

- Preparing questions before a client meeting.
- Turning rough notes into a short list of next steps.
- Outlining a weekly update from facts you already have.
- Planning a few content ideas around a topic you know.
- Improving a follow-up email you have already drafted.

Choose something with a result you can review during the session. “Help me organize these notes” gives you a clearer starting point than “automate all of my administration.” A larger idea can come later, once you have practiced a useful part of it.

If nothing comes to mind, try one of [these three everyday work exercises](/learn/everyday-work-tasks-to-practice-with-ai). You are welcome to begin with an example rather than your own material.

## Bring a small, appropriate example

You might bring a paragraph of notes, the structure of a report, or a draft with the private details removed. Follow your organization’s rules about using workplace information with AI tools. When in doubt, use fictional names and details.

For example, replace a real client’s notes with:

```text
I need to follow up after a project meeting.
The client reviewed the draft and asked for a simpler layout.
We still need their photos. We have not agreed on a deadline.
I want a short email that confirms the next step.
```

That is enough to practice giving context, asking for a draft, and checking whether it invents a commitment. You do not need to bring the entire client file.

<Callout type="tip" title="A rough example is welcome">
  If you are unsure how to explain the task, bring what you have. Part of the session is learning to
  turn a rough request into instructions an AI assistant can use.
</Callout>

## Decide what would make the result useful

Before the workshop, finish this sentence: “I would be happy if I could leave knowing how to…”

Your answer might be “turn my notes into an action list” or “ask better follow-up questions when an AI answer is too general.” Keep it specific. This gives you a useful question to ask and something to practice, without expecting to finish an entire business system in 90 minutes.

If you have time, read [how to get more useful answers from AI](/learn/get-better-answers-from-ai). You can also arrive without reading anything first. The workshop is designed to include questions and step-by-step help.

## Get your laptop ready

Charge your laptop and check that you can sign in to the AI assistant you usually use. If signing in or choosing a tool is where you are stuck, bring that question too. Avoid planning your exercise around a paid feature you do not already have.

Keep your example somewhere easy to find. A short note in a document is sufficient. Bring a charger, and leave room to take a few notes about the instructions and follow-up questions that helped.
