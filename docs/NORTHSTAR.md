# Accelerate Agent-Native Business Runtime

## Product, Architecture, and Implementation Specification

## 1. Purpose

Accelerate should evolve from a customizable business Command Center into an **agent-native business runtime**.

The system should provide the durable business infrastructure that humans, AI agents, plugins, and dynamically generated interfaces all operate against.

The core product thesis is:

> **Accelerate is the open-source operating layer for AI-native businesses. It connects the company's systems, maintains a trustworthy model of what is happening, and gives AI coworkers a governed environment for doing real work.**

This means Accelerate is not primarily:

* a CRM
* a chatbot
* a dashboard
* an automation builder
* a collection of AI features
* a fixed SaaS application

Those can all exist on top of Accelerate.

The durable value is the underlying runtime:

* business entities and relationships
* identity
* activity history
* communications
* business state
* integrations
* permissions
* knowledge
* evidence
* tasks
* agent execution
* approvals
* audit history
* workflows
* plugin capabilities

The UI is one interface into that runtime.

AI agents are another.

External agents using MCP are another.

Custom applications generated by coding agents are another.

---

# 2. Design assumption

Accelerate should be designed around the assumption that **software generation becomes extremely cheap**.

AI coding will increasingly commoditize:

* CRUD interfaces
* dashboards
* forms
* internal applications
* bespoke workflows
* simple integrations
* reporting views
* frontend customization

Therefore Accelerate should not derive its defensibility from owning one fixed interface.

Instead, Accelerate should provide the parts that generated software still needs:

* authoritative business state
* secure identity
* tenant boundaries
* persistent memory
* integrations
* permissions
* transaction safety
* reliable actions
* provenance
* auditability
* agent governance
* scheduling
* event handling
* business primitives

An AI coding system should eventually be able to receive:

> Build me an operations screen showing technician utilization, callback rates, overdue estimates, and today's urgent jobs.

and generate that interface on top of Accelerate without rebuilding authentication, CRM entities, permissions, integrations, AI context, or task execution.

Accelerate should make AI coding **more powerful**, not compete with it.

# 3. Product model

The system should be understood as five layers.

## Layer 1: SEE

Accelerate observes the business.

Examples:

* customers
* contacts
* companies
* opportunities
* inbound leads
* Gmail
* Outlook
* calendar
* Drive
* website activity
* payments
* invoices
* proposals
* campaigns
* support conversations
* user notes
* external integrations

The objective is to create a normalized model of business reality.

This layer answers:

> What happened?

---

## Layer 2: REMEMBER

Accelerate maintains durable organizational memory.

This includes:

* canonical records
* entity relationships
* activity history
* indexed knowledge
* communication history
* provenance
* source references
* human notes
* business facts
* AI-derived observations
* evidence supporting those observations

This layer answers:

> What do we know, and why do we believe it?

---

## Layer 3: NOTICE

Accelerate continuously evaluates business state and identifies what deserves attention.

Examples:

* a lead has not received a reply
* an important proposal is stale
* an invoice is overdue
* close rate dropped sharply
* a customer appears unhappy
* a meeting is approaching
* a campaign is underperforming
* a promised follow-up was never completed
* an opportunity has gone dormant
* an integration stopped syncing

This layer answers:

> What matters now?

---

## Layer 4: ACT

Accelerate can perform or prepare real business work.

Examples:

* draft a reply
* send an approved email
* update a CRM record
* prepare a proposal
* create a follow-up
* schedule a meeting
* send an invoice reminder
* prepare a report
* generate a customer brief
* update a campaign
* trigger an external workflow
* invoke another business system

This layer must always operate through explicit tools, permissions, policies, validation, receipts, and audit history.

This layer answers:

> What should happen next, and can the system safely do it?

---

## Layer 5: LEARN

Accelerate learns from repeated human behavior.

This must not mean unconstrained model self-modification.

It should instead observe explicit signals such as:

* approvals
* denials
* draft edits
* priority overrides
* repeated workflow decisions
* trusted evidence sources
* repeated manual actions

Then it can propose explicit rules.

Example:

> You have approved this exact category of overdue invoice reminder 23 times. Allow the Finance Coworker to send these automatically?

The user remains in control.

This layer answers:

> Which repeated decisions can become explicit policy?

# 4. Core product primitive: Coworkers

Accelerate should introduce **Coworkers** as first-class objects.

A Coworker is not a separate LLM instance or chatbot.

A Coworker is a configuration over the shared Accelerate runtime.

A Coworker consists of:

* identity
* role
* objectives
* skills
* available tools
* connected capabilities
* triggers
* permissions
* autonomy policies
* memory scope
* allowed entities
* budgets
* schedules
* escalation rules

Examples:

### Sales Coworker

Responsible for:

* lead qualification
* follow-up detection
* opportunity research
* proposal follow-up
* meeting preparation
* pipeline hygiene

### Finance Coworker

Responsible for:

* overdue invoices
* payment anomalies
* cash visibility
* recurring revenue changes
* financial summaries

### Operations Coworker

Responsible for:

* task monitoring
* customer commitments
* scheduling issues
* operational exceptions

### Marketing Coworker

Responsible for:

Do not create separate copies of CRM, memory, permissions, or integrations for each coworker.

Conceptual example:

```yaml
name: Sales Coworker

  Monitors leads, opportunities, proposals and customer
  communication and prepares or executes appropriate follow-up.

skills:
  - qualify-lead
  - research-opportunity

  - email.send

  - lead.created
  - proposal.stale
```

The important requirement is that worker configuration becomes explicit, inspectable, portable, and eventually shareable.

# 6. Durable Work Engine

Autonomous behavior must not be implemented as:

```text
cron → prompt → hope
```

Work itself must be represented durably.

Introduce a generalized `WorkItem` or equivalent abstraction.

Suggested conceptual fields:

```text
id
tenantId
coworkerId
kind
objective
entityType
entityId
priority
reason
source
status
dueAt
nextCheckAt
leaseOwner
leaseExpiresAt
attemptCount
maxAttempts
createdAt
startedAt
finishedAt
outcome
error
runId
```

A WorkItem represents something that needs to happen.

Examples:

```text
Follow up with Acme proposal
Research new inbound lead
Prepare John for 2 PM meeting
Review overdue invoices
Check whether customer replied
Generate weekly revenue report
```

## Required properties

### Durable

The item survives:

* browser closure
* deployment
* process restart
* model failure
* agent failure

### Lease-based

Multiple workers must not process the same item simultaneously.

Use transactional claims or equivalent safe locking.

A common pattern is:

```sql
FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED
```

or an equivalent atomic execution claim.

### Retryable

Failure should not automatically lose the work.

Retries should be explicit and bounded.

### Schedulable

The worker should be able to schedule its own future work.

Example:

```text
nextCheckAt: Friday 9 AM
reason: "Proposal remains unanswered after first follow-up."
```

### Explainable

Every scheduled future action must carry a reason.

No unexplained:

```text
check again in 14 days
```

The system should know why.

---

# 7. Separate triggers from work

Triggers create or update WorkItems.

They should not contain complex agent reasoning.

Examples:

```text
email.received
lead.created
meeting.upcoming
proposal.sent
invoice.overdue
calendar.event.ended
campaign.completed
scheduled.tick
```

The trigger can deterministically create work:

```text
lead.created
→ WorkItem(kind=qualify_lead)
```

The Coworker decides what the work means.

This separation keeps event infrastructure simple and intelligence replaceable.

---

# 8. Deterministic software before AI

LLMs should only be used when judgment, synthesis, interpretation, or language generation is actually necessary.

Do not use models for things SQL or deterministic code can answer.

Bad:

> Ask an LLM which invoices are 30 days overdue.

Correct:

```sql
SELECT invoices
WHERE due_at < NOW() - INTERVAL '30 days'
AND status = 'unpaid'
```

Then AI may answer:

* which customers should be contacted first?
* what tone should we use?
* is there context suggesting we should wait?
* should this be escalated?

General principle:

> **Software determines facts. Models handle ambiguity.**

This should be enforced throughout the architecture.

# 9. Capability Graph

Every workspace should expose its available capabilities in one canonical system.

This extends the existing integration capability platform.

Examples:

```text
crm.read                 available
crm.write                available
gmail.read               available
gmail.send               available
calendar.read            available
calendar.write           available
drive.search             available
stripe.read              unavailable
quickbooks.read          unavailable
sms.send                 unavailable
```

Capabilities may also encode policy:

```text
email.send:
  available: true
  policy: approval_required
```

A Coworker should know its capabilities before beginning work.

It should not discover missing integrations by repeatedly failing calls.

# 10. Plugin architecture

Plugins should build on Accelerate capabilities rather than bypass them.

A plugin can provide:

* tools
* skills
* workflows
* triggers
* UI
* connectors
* coworker definitions

Conceptual structure:

```text
plugins/
  meeting-intelligence/
    plugin.json
    skills/
    tools/
    workflows/
    triggers/
    permissions/
    ui/
```

Plugins should declare required capabilities.

Example:

```yaml
requires:
  - crm.read
  - crm.write
  - calendar.read
  - email.read
```

If requirements are unavailable, the plugin should show itself as partially or fully unavailable rather than failing unpredictably.

# 12. Tool Registry

All agent actions must go through a canonical Tool Registry.

Tools should expose narrow business actions.

Examples:

```text
crm.read_contact
crm.update_contact
crm.read_company
crm.read_opportunity

email.read_thread
email.create_draft
email.send_draft

calendar.read_event
calendar.create_event

proposal.read
proposal.create

tasks.create
tasks.complete

knowledge.search
```

Avoid generic unrestricted primitives when a constrained business primitive is possible.

Bad:

```text
run arbitrary SQL
```

Better:

```text
crm.update_opportunity_stage
```

Bad:

```text
raw Gmail API request
```

Better:

```text
email.send_draft
```

This is required for governance, observability, and safety.

Every entity read should return useful references to related records.

Example:

### Contact read

Return:

* contact ID
* company ID
* opportunity IDs
* communication IDs
* meeting IDs
* proposal IDs
* task IDs

### Company read

Return:

* company ID

* opportunity IDs
* communication history
* meetings
* proposals

### Opportunity read

Return:

* company

* stage history


# 14. Evidence and Claim Ledger

Do not allow models to invent their own confidence scores and treat them as truth.

Create an evidence-backed fact system.

Conceptual model:

```text
Claim
- id
- tenant
- entity
- field/concept
- proposedValue
- status

Evidence
- claimId
- sourceType
- sourceId
- observation
- timestamp
- provenance
- strength
```

Example:

```text
Claim:
Susan works at Acme

Evidence:
- Gmail signature:
  "Susan Lee, VP Sales, Acme"

- LinkedIn:
  Current employer: Acme

Decision:
VERIFIED
```

The model reports observations.

Deterministic business rules determine whether those observations are strong enough to update authoritative state.

---

# 15. Human truth protection

AI should not casually overwrite data entered or explicitly confirmed by humans.

Suggested hierarchy:

```text
Human-confirmed
Human-entered
Verified external evidence
Probable external evidence
Model inference
```

Rules may differ by field, but the general principle should be:

### Blank field

Strong sourced evidence can often fill automatically.

### Existing machine-generated value

Stronger evidence may replace it.

### Existing human-entered value

AI should normally create a suggestion or conflict rather than overwrite.

### Explicit human-confirmed value

Only extremely strong evidence or human review should change it.

---

# 16. Autonomy Policy Engine

Accelerate needs one coherent system governing agent actions.

This should unify:

* AI confirmations
* automation permissions
* standing approvals
* coworker permissions
* safety floors
* audit provenance

Use an autonomy ladder.

## Level 0: Prohibited

The agent cannot perform the action.

Example:

```text
delete_financial_history
```

## Level 1: Always ask

Every action requires approval.

Example:

```text
issue_refund
```

## Level 2: Ask until trusted

The action initially requires approval.

The user may later grant standing permission.

Example:

```text
send_followup_email
```

## Level 3: Standing permission

The action can execute automatically within explicit constraints.

Example:

```text
Send routine follow-ups
only to existing customers
only using approved template categories
maximum 20/day
```

## Level 4: Autonomous read/reason

Low-risk operations can execute freely.

Example:

```text
read CRM
search knowledge
analyze pipeline
prepare drafts
```

---

# 17. Hard safety floors

Certain actions should never become automatically executable simply because users repeatedly approve them.

Examples may include:

* destructive account deletion
* credential changes
* deleting financial history
* exporting an entire customer database
* high-value refunds
* major financial transfers
* dangerous system operations

These floors must exist in code.

They must not be removable by prompt instructions.

---

# 18. Approval provenance

Every consequential action should record:

```text
who initiated it
which coworker
which WorkItem
which tool
what inputs
what evidence
what policy applied
whether human approved it
whether standing policy approved it
when it executed
what happened
```

Example:

```text
Email sent
Coworker: Sales
Reason: Proposal unanswered for five days
Generated from: CRM + Gmail thread
Approval: standing policy #12
Tool: email.send_draft
Receipt: xyz
```

The audit trail should answer:

> Who did this, why did it happen, and what authorized it?

---

# 19. Agent Run Traces

Every agent run should have a durable trace.

A run should record:

```text
runId
coworker
objective
WorkItem
context version
tools used
tool outputs
claims generated
actions proposed
actions executed
errors
token/model usage
cost
duration
outcome
```

User-facing views should show a readable summary.

Internal logs can contain greater technical detail.

Do not expose raw model reasoning as a requirement.

The important user-level explanation is:

* what it looked at
* what it concluded
* why
* what it did
* what happens next

Example:

# 23. Memory architecture

Memory should not be treated as a single vector database.

There are different categories.

## Canonical memory

Actual business records.

Examples:

* contact
* company
* opportunity
* invoice
* meeting
* campaign

## Activity memory

What happened.

Examples:

* email
* call
* note
* stage change
* task completion

## Knowledge memory

Documents and indexed sources.

Examples:

* Drive
* PDFs
* policies
* proposals
* playbooks

## Agent memory

Agent-specific context.

Examples:

* prior work
* prior research
* scheduled checks
* unresolved questions

## Learned policy

Explicit rules derived from human decisions.

These categories should not be casually collapsed together.

# 24. Budgets

Autonomous workers require explicit resource constraints.

Support per-worker or per-tenant limits such as:

```text
maximum model spend/day
maximum vendor API calls
maximum emails/day
maximum research depth
maximum retry count
maximum runtime
```

Running out of budget should be a normal state.

Example:

```text
Sales research budget exhausted.
3 low-priority leads deferred until tomorrow.
```

It should not appear as an unexplained agent failure.

Accelerate Core must be useful independently of Accelerate's branded implementation.

A developer should be able to:


* logging
* scheduling
* AI gateway

# 36. Core architectural principles

Every developer should understand these.

## Principle 1

**The database/business runtime is authoritative. The model is not.**

---

## Principle 2

**Agents use tools. They do not bypass business services.**

---

## Principle 3

**Every consequential action has an explicit authorization path.**

---

## Principle 4

**Every autonomous action must be explainable after the fact.**

---

## Principle 5

**Every AI-derived fact should retain provenance.**

---

## Principle 6

**Deterministic logic should remain deterministic.**

---

## Principle 7

**AI may propose policy. Humans establish policy.**

---

## Principle 8

**Plugins extend Accelerate rather than create parallel infrastructure.**

---

## Principle 9

**Capabilities are explicit. Missing capabilities are normal.**

---

## Principle 10

**Work is durable. A browser session is not the unit of execution.**

# 37. What success looks like

Accelerate should eventually support this interaction:

Owner:

> Keep an eye on every proposal over $10,000. If they haven't responded within four business days, review the account and previous communication, draft an appropriate follow-up, and send it automatically unless the customer has expressed concern or there is an unresolved issue.

Accelerate should translate that into:

* explicit policy
* trigger
* WorkItems
* entity queries
* context retrieval
* deterministic eligibility rules
* AI judgment where necessary
* email draft
* authorization
* action receipt
* future scheduling
* audit trail

The owner should be able to inspect and revoke the behavior at any time.

That is the target product.

Not:

> Here's a chatbot that can answer questions about your CRM.
